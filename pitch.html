Why nothing quite like this exists

Every heat tool stops at the parking lot fence. This one doesn't.

Search for "stadium heat risk" and you get city-level heat indices, generic weather widgets, or a single WBGT number pinned to a stadium's coordinates. None of them ask the actual operational question a venue manager has: which specific lot, how full, how hot is the walk from the car.

The instrument, in three moments

Screenshots from the live, deployed site — not mockups.

01 · The map

Eleven real stadiums, ranked live

A self-hosted US basemap (no map API key) with all 11 host cities colored by our own percentile-based heat-risk scale. Drag the month/hour scrubber and every marker recolors from 2.46M real readings — no page reload.

  • Own percentile risk scale, not a borrowed NWS number we couldn't verify
  • Real WBGT (Wet-Bulb Globe Temperature) — the actual metric US sports medicine uses
02 · The 3D model

Real astronomy. Real geometry. Nobody else's 3D model does either.

The sun's position comes from real orbital mechanics (SunCalc) for this exact stadium's lat/lon and the selected moment — verified against an independent calculation. The 11 parking lots around it are drawn at their real OpenStreetMap-measured shape and compass orientation, not a generic scatter.

  • Click any lot → camera flies to it
  • Select a real upcoming game → cars fill in at the exact real stall count
03 · Zoom all the way in

1,000 real spaces means 1,000 cars, not a sample

Every rendered car is one real stall at its real position — not a decorative fill. Fill a lot to 40% and exactly 40% of its real stalls light up. We hit real WebGL and React-reconciliation limits building this and fixed both live, documented in the repo.

04 · Ask it anything

An LLM that narrates. Formulas that measure.

"Which stadium is the highest heat risk in July at 3pm?" — the agent calls a real tool, gets a real number (Hard Rock Stadium, 32.75°C, n=759 real readings), and states it. It cannot answer any other way. Every answer's "data used" trace is one click away.

Not a dashboard — a discovery

Three findings this pipeline actually computed, not asserted.

Finding 01
MetLife Stadium is warming at +1.15°C per decade — the fastest of all 11 venues.

Real linear regression on 20 years of July WBGT. SoFi Stadium is the one honest exception: -0.17°C/decade, reported plainly rather than hidden.

Finding 02
The real 2026 tournament ran hotter than its own 20-year normal — independently, not by restating the trend.

54 of 78 real matches ran above their stadium's own climatology mean for that exact hour. A live validation the trend actually held, computed from a completely separate real dataset (Iowa State Mesonet ASOS).

Finding 03
A 117.4m × 35.7m real parking lot yields exactly 126 real spaces — not an estimate.

Rotate the lot's real OpenStreetMap boundary into its own long axis, fit real 9×18 ft stalls and a real 24 ft two-way aisle as double-loaded rows. Checked by hand against the formula.

The discipline underneath it

Every number on the site carries its own receipt.

Four data tiers, applied without exception across 11 tools, six data pipelines, and every card in the UI:

REAL — measured or observed REAL-FORECAST — live NWS prediction SEMI — real geometry, modeled formula MOCK — a stated assumption

The Ask agent's system prompt makes this a hard rule, not a style guide: it may only state a number a tool call actually returned, and must say "not yet verified" rather than fill a gap with something plausible. Caught in testing: asked which section of Mercedes-Benz Stadium gets the most sun, it correctly refused — that stadium's field orientation is still real OpenStreetMap data pending, tagged TODO-SEMI, not guessed.

"AI doesn't measure. Formulas measure. AI narrates what the formulas already found — and says so."

A discipline carried over from an earlier project on this same team, applied here to eleven live function-calling tools instead of one offline narrative pass — and stress-tested with a real, reproduced WebGL buffer-overflow bug and a real 10-second render freeze, both found and fixed by measuring, not guessing.
